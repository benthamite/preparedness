# Mobility: displacement, go-bag, evacuation, car (rows 26, 40, 90–95)

Headline: displacement is costly, but nothing in this group is shown to change it much. No study measures whether a packed bag or a plan gets people out sooner, and the two US wildfire regressions I read point the other way. Every effect size is a guess with a low end near zero.

## The displacement need

**Hardship, days 2–7: $90 per person-day (35–230).**

- Money, $50. Surveys cited in Jiang et al. 2023 (read): $88 per evacuating household per day for Lili (2002), $83 for Katrina and Rita (2005). At about 1.85× and 1.7× inflation (CPI from memory) that is $160 and $140, or $54–63 per person at 2.6 people. Ike's $350 a day is an outlier.
- Bottom-up check: 27% in hotels (median of hurricane studies) × ($113–178 room ÷ 2.5 + $25 food) = $20–26; 62% hosted × $10 = $6; fuel $10. Total $38–44.
- FEMA's method (read) is one GSA room per family plus meals per diem less $9 food at home: the all-hotel case, about $105 per person.
- Lost income $10 and discomfort $30: guesses.
- Cross-check: Gellman et al. 2026 (read) put the welfare cost of evacuating, before lodging and food, at $117 per person at Category 3 and $343 at Category 4–5. The curve gives $330 for three days.

Day 1 is $150: the journey out and back. Later steps ($80, $60, $35, $15 from days 8, 31, 91, 181) are guesses.

Weighted by Household Pulse durations (read: 43% under a week, 25% to a month, 11% to six months, 7% longer, 14% never returned), the curve gives 0.43×330 + 0.25×1,250 + 0.11×5,230 + 0.07×10,630 + 0.14×12,055 ≈ $3,460 per displaced adult. At 1.3% a year that is $45 per adult per year. The first three days are about 10% of it.

**Mortality, healthy adult: 1.5 micromorts on day 1, 0.1 a day after, 0.15 after a month.** All guesses.

- Day 1: 250 extra miles × 0.005 micromorts per occupant-mile (1.19 deaths per 100M vehicle-miles × 0.63 occupant share ÷ 1.5 occupants; snippet and memory) = 1.2, rounded up.
- Check against Rita, the worst US evacuation: about 107 deaths, less 51 care-home residents, leaves 56 among roughly 3 million, 19 micromorts per evacuee. The curve with multipliers gives a four-day evacuation 0.6×1.8 + 0.2×1.3 + 0.13×9 + 0.06×54 ≈ 5.8: plausible for an ordinary evacuation, possibly high.

**Multipliers.** Over-75s ×30 for mortality (range 8–100): if four fifths of Rita's 56 were 65+ (13% of evacuees), their risk was about 115 micromorts against about 4 for the rest. About 90% of Fukushima's 1,656 indirect deaths were 66 or older (read). Care-home residents are a different order: mortality 2.68 times higher after the Fukushima evacuation (Nomura, read on PLOS); 90-day death risk up 2.7–5.3 points in four Gulf hurricanes (Dosa, abstract, wording ambiguous). Other multipliers are guesses.

## What preparation changes

Read in full:

- Marshall Fire (Forrister 2024, n=191): delay from awareness to departure averaged 137 minutes; preparation after deciding, 64. Each preparation minute added 0.70 minutes of delay. 70% had no plan; having one did not predict leaving.
- Camp Fire (Grajdura 2021): mean awareness 8:00, mean departure 9:33. Knowing the community plan meant hearing 19 minutes sooner, but not shorter preparation (+11 minutes, not significant) or earlier departure (+18, not significant).
- Camp Fire deaths: 85 in about 38,000 residents, mean age 72.
- Late evacuation: 48% of Australian bushfire deaths 1901–2011.
- Sandy (step 2): kit and plan did not reduce adverse outcomes.

Delay kills, deciding takes longer than packing, and prepared households have not been observed leaving sooner.

## Items

| Entry | Annual cost | Effect (median) |

| Go-bag, $130 a person | $32 a person | −4% displacement hardship; −4% wildfire and flood deaths |

| Half-tank habit | $20 | −1% hardship; −1.5% deaths; leaving 3 points likelier |
| Car kit, $90 | $21 | −0.5% deaths; value is everyday |
| Get-home bag, $45 | $16 | $0.15 a year |

| Tent or tarp, $100 | $13 | −6% hardship in earthquakes, −1% elsewhere |

Costs use 3% and $20 an hour, after the everyday share.

- **Go-bag, hardship.** Leaving with nothing costs perhaps $50 a person: 15% of the first three days' $330. Those days are 10–40% of displacement harm, so 0.015–0.06; median 0.04. With half a day's warning anyone packs a bag, hence late-buy class staple.
- **Go-bag, deaths.** If packing is a third of the 64 minutes and the bag halves it, departure is 11 × 0.7 ≈ 8 minutes sooner, under a tenth of the usual 90–140. Taken to remove 4% of caught-by-the-fire deaths (0–15%).
- **Costs.** Go-bag: $130 × 0.142 × 0.85 = $16, plus household time and upkeep ($11 + $20 + $8) ÷ 2.5 = $16. Plan: 3 hours × $20 × 0.218 = $13, plus half an hour a year.
- **Plan, hardship.** 27% pay for rooms; a standing invitation moves a third of them, 9% of households, saving $65 of $90: 6.5%, plus 2% for knowing where to go. That 8% holds for the first month, 35–70% of harm: 0.03–0.055.
- **Plan, deaths.** Triggers attack the deciding; a destination removes a minority reason for staying (Harvard 2007: 31% would not leave; home safe 75%, roads 56%, pets 27%). The null results keep the low end at zero.
- **Half tank, cost.** 460 gallons a year is 44 fills at a quarter tank or 66 at a half; 22 extra stops × 4 minutes ≈ 1.5 hours, set at 1 because many already refuel early. The habit is not free.
- **Half tank, benefit.** A third of drivers are below half; about 40% of those meet dry pumps in a mass evacuation (Irma: 50–60% of stations out in Florida metros, read). So 13% of households lose one to three hours: about $5 on $330.
- **Car kit.** 27 million AAA calls (read) over about 0.7 trillion member-miles is one per 26,000 miles: 0.6% on a 150-mile leg, perhaps 2% in an evacuation, 40% curable.
- **Tent.** A fifth of quake-displaced households without a nearby host × $30 of $90 saved = 6%.

At these medians none of the seven reaches a ratio of 1 for an average US household. The plan comes closest on a hurricane coast, where displacement is four to eight times likelier.
