### Arguments that AI raises the risk during the transition

Nobody in this list gives a probability of war.

- **Aschenbrenner** (Situational Awareness IIId, June 2024, V): the advantage would be "decisive enough even to preemptively take out an adversary's nuclear deterrent"; "at least initially, the incentives for first-strikes will be tremendous"; "there is a real chance that the AGI endgame plays out with the backdrop of world war".
- **Hendrycks, Schmidt and Wang** (Superintelligence Strategy, March 2025, V): "States will act to disable threatening AI projects", up to "Kinetic attacks on datacenters"; "A 'transparent ocean' would threaten submarine stealth"; "The typical outcome of a Superintelligence Manhattan Project is extreme escalation". Critics: Rehman, Mueller and Mazarr at RAND (13 Mar 2025, V) call the balance "highly unstable", with "potent first-strike incentives"; Wildeford and Delaney (2 Apr 2025, V) say "MAIM lacks the characteristics that made nuclear deterrence effective". Hendrycks and Khoja reply (22 Sep 2025, V) that it "reduces the volatility that ASI development introduces".
- **AI 2027, race branch** (3 Apr 2025, V): in mid-2026 Party members weigh "A blockade of Taiwan? A full invasion?"; in August 2027 the White House asks for "a plan for kinetic attacks on Chinese datacenters", and "TSMC is still the source of more than 80% of American AI chips". The scenario contains no US-China war and no nuclear use; an arms build-up ends in a 2029 deal, and its catastrophe is extinction in 2030, outside this model.
- **Forethought.** MacAskill and Moorhouse (11 Mar 2025, V): a technology explosion "could disrupt the current balance of power, in a way that could trigger war", and an industrial explosion could bring "dramatically larger nuclear stockpiles".
- **Shulman** (Dwarkesh, 26 Jun 2023, V): a power left out of the expansion "would be helpless before another one that had undergone it"; he lists "The ability to find hidden nuclear subs". **Karnofsky** (80,000 Hours, July 2025, V) wants to "get through this AI transition without big changes in the balance of power".
- **RAND, GovAI, CNAS.** Mitre and Predd (10 Feb 2025, V) list "a wonder weapon with a significant first-mover-advantage". Patell and Guest (GovAI and IAPS, 20 Feb 2026, V): adversaries "may be tempted to engage in preventive conflict". Stokes (CNAS, 27 Aug 2026, V): the race could "increase the risk of military conflict or disaster in multiple ways".
- **Amodei** (January 2026, V) is "not totally sure we can be confident in the nuclear deterrent against a country of geniuses in a datacenter".
- **AI in warning and intelligence; decision time.** Sentinel (21 Sep 2026, V) reports that the US nearly boarded a Chinese ship because of an erroneous AI-assisted intelligence report. Winter-Levy notes that the moves states make to protect their forces "might shore up second-strike capabilities, but also increase the risk of accident" (V). Biden and Xi affirmed human control over nuclear launch decisions in November 2024 (S).

Most of these predate 2026. Since then an Iran war began (28 Feb), a Trump-Xi summit extended the trade truce (September), and METR judged that Anthropic had probably reached a twofold speed-up of AI research; Sentinel says the AI Futures Project now expects a superhuman AI researcher before 2028 (all from Sentinel, V; not checked at source).

### Arguments that it lowers the risk or leaves it unchanged

- **Deterrence holds.** Winter-Levy and Lalwani (Carnegie; 80,000 Hours, recorded 24 Nov 2025, V): "ballistic missile submarines are likely to remain reliable second-strike nuclear forces"; "missile defence remains a domain that is really tilted against the defender". They add that the result is "still a very destabilising, potentially scary world".
- **War stays unlikely.** Burdette and Demelash (RAND, 2025, abstract V) find the absolute probability low: "preventive war appears relatively more likely to occur in an attempt to preserve a monopoly on AGI than to prevent one". Stokes: AGI "would not automatically convert to geopolitical dominance".
- **Agreement gets easier.** MacAskill and Moorhouse: "third-party 'treaty bots' could be made to verifiably enforce an agreement". Shulman prefers "an international deal that can regulate the pace of progress" but warns that verifying a rival's AI is hard. The September 2026 Trump-Xi summit produced an AI incident channel (Sentinel, V).
- **Symmetry.** Cotton-Barratt (11 Jun 2024, V) expects the risk to rise, but grants that on an outside view the effect "is equally likely to result in, say, a 10x decrease in risk as a 10x increase".
- **Failed predictions.** General Minihan's 2023 memo: "My gut tells me we will fight in 2025" (S). Polymarket's price for an invasion by end-2026 fell from about 13% in January 2026 (S) to 1% (V). I found no systematic record of such predictions.
- **Slow diffusion.** Narayanan and Kapoor (15 Apr 2025, V): "there is no straightforward reason to expect arms races between countries"; "Diffusion occurs over decades, not years". They exclude military AI.

### Domestic political violence

Quantified: Davidson, asked on AXRP (7 Aug 2025, V) about the risk "that the United States gets AI-enabled couped": "if I had to pluck a number, I'd say 10%, but it's very made up", with no horizon. The Forethought paper (Davidson, Finnveden and Hadshar, 15 Apr 2025, V) gives no number. Karnofsky rejects 50% for a human power grab and has no plan to get it "below 1%". Amodei: "AI will disrupt 50% of entry-level white-collar jobs over 1–5 years". Not quantified by anyone I found: the chance that job losses cause serious unrest, the net effect of AI surveillance, and how violent a seizure of power would be (Davidson: "Probably not going to shoot everyone, but going to make them go home").

## 2. What I infer

Annualised (1 − (1 − p)^(1/years), from 19 Nov 2026 unless dated):

- US-China war: Q8362 gives 1.0% a year. Q11480 × Q11112 = 23% × 57% = 13%, or 1.7% a year. FutureSearch gives 2.4%. I use **1.3%**.
- Russia-NATO war: Q8636 gives 1.7% a year, Q7452 0.36%. I use **1.0%**.
- Any nuclear war: Rodriguez 1.1%; Q4779 1.8%; Q3150 3.3%; Good Judgment (2018, via Rodriguez) 0.40%. I use **1.2%**.
- Nuclear warfare involving the US: Rodriguez's 0.38% shaded toward superforecasters (0.3%) plus half of ONN's 0.27% a year for US-China: **0.45%**. NATO-Russia outside a crisis: **0.3%**.

Baseline is today's forecast with AI's contribution removed, so that the Iran war and the lapse of New START are not blamed on AI. I take AI's share of present US-China risk as 10% and of nuclear and unrest risk as about 10%; these are guesses.

## 3. My guesses

- **Transition.** No source quantifies it. I multiply the US-China war rate by 3, the Russia-NATO rate by 1.5, great-power nuclear risk by 2.5 and any nuclear use by 2. A war rate of 4% a year is 18% over five years, which I place between RAND's "low" and Aschenbrenner's "real chance". The decade after 1945, the nearest precedent for a technology that upset the military balance, saw one direct US-China war (R). The low end is the sceptic who sees no AI effect; the high end is about 47% over five years for war and Cotton-Barratt's tenfold increase for nuclear use.
- **civilUnrest, US transition**: 0.7% + 0.5% (Davidson's 10% spread over five years, × 0.25 that it disrupts the household) + 0.8% for unrest over jobs = 2%.
- **After**: a third to a half of today's rates for war and nuclear hazards, with a wide range.
- **survivableShare**: nuclearNear 0.6 (0.3–0.85); nuclearFar 0.9 (0.92 in Buenos Aires); greatPowerWar 0.97; civilUnrest 0.95.

## 5. Geography

- **Large US city.** CSIS's wargame summary (V) puts the losses of a US-China war on ships, aircraft and "tens of thousands of servicemembers" and mentions no strike on the mainland; I assume none. The household meets cyberattack on utilities, for which CISA says China is pre-positioned (V), shortages of electronics and some drugs, and a first-year loss near 10% of world output (Bloomberg Economics, S). In the nuclear case the US is the prime target.
- **Western European city.** A US-China war arrives as a supply and financial shock. A Russia-NATO war adds sabotage, strikes on infrastructure and possible conscription.
- **Buenos Aires.** No attack, no conscription, no fallout. War arrives through trade (China took 11.3% of exports in January to November 2025, S) and finance: I guess a 0.3 chance of exchange controls or a bank holiday in the year of a US-China war. Argentina exports food, so no food window opens. Nuclear war adds global cooling; Xia et al. 2022 was not read.

The JSON notes give windows for greatPowerWar in each place; all are guesses.

## 6. Not opened, and weaknesses

Not opened: Metaculus question pages and API (403; embeds only, so criteria unread); polymarket.com; web.archive.org; the EA Forum (429), so the XPT figures and Samotsvety's October 2022 update; the FRI and ONN nuclear PDF (too large; landing page read); Xia et al. 2022; the full RAND report by Burdette and Demelash; the ODNI assessment; Bloomberg Economics; the audio in which Davidson gives five- and thirty-year estimates.

Weaknesses. The transition multipliers rest on qualitative arguments. Quotations came through a fetch tool that summarises pages; I rechecked those from Aschenbrenner, AI 2027 and Davidson with a second fetch, not the rest. A blockade without war is likelier than war on these forecasts and is not counted. The Metaculus questions disagree with each other by a factor of about two. The evidence on failed predictions of war is thin.
